# Shared brief: user-experience research (Nov 2026)

Goal: find out what REAL USERS experience with AI tools, as of November 2026. The site owner wants the atlas tags (recommended, popular, cheap, worthwhile free plan, stands out) to rest on user experience and reviews, NOT on the vendor's website and NOT mainly on "best of" listicles (an earlier round already covered professional roundups; see /home/user/ai-atlas/research/community/all-categories-report.md section of your categories for which tools lead there, and use that to pick the tools to check).

Prioritize, in this order:
1. Review platforms with real user text: Trustpilot, G2, Capterra, GetApp, Software Advice, Product Hunt reviews. Record score, number of reviews, date read, AND the 2-4 most repeated praise and complaint themes in the review text (with share of 1-star where shown).
2. App store data: Apple App Store and Google Play rating, rating count, recent review themes.
3. First-person experience: Hacker News threads (news.ycombinator.com, hn.algolia.com search), Medium / Substack / personal blog posts "I used X for N months", official community forums (e.g. community.openai.com, forum.figma.com, Adobe community), Discord or X posts quoted in articles, Quora.
4. YouTube: video titles, channels and view counts found through search (transcripts are unreachable from here); note the verdict only if the title or description states it.
5. Surveys of users (developer or creator surveys) where they exist.

Do NOT use: Reddit (the site owner decided not to use it at all), diyai.io, vendor pages as evidence of quality. Vendor pages may be used only to confirm a plan or price, and must be labelled as vendor.

For each tool: verdict from users (loved / mixed / frustrated), top praise, top complaints, who it fits (beginner, designer, pro), whether users say the free plan is usable, whether users call it good value, any trust issues (billing, refunds, data, bans), and Hebrew support if any user mentions it.

Rules: every claim has a URL and the date read; mark READ (seen on the page) or INFERRED (from a title or snippet); mark unverified items "Assumption"; flag anything older than 6 months as STALE; never invent numbers, ratings or quotes; distinguish one user's opinion from a repeated pattern. Known limits: search results often return titles only; PCMag and CNET block the fetcher; Reddit is out. A shared fetch quota of about 100 per hour is used by other researchers too: write your notes file early and update it after every few fetches so nothing is lost if you are cut off.
